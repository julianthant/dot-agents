# Persuasive copy

Use only when the brief asks for persuasion, positioning or conversion copy.

Read supplied product context and existing approved claims before asking for more. Establish who the
reader is, their problem, the offer, the evidence and the desired next action. Resolve only missing
facts that would change the copy. Do not impose a marketing intake on a simple email edit.

Lead with a specific reader benefit that the evidence supports. Explain the mechanism or concrete
feature behind it. Address a material objection when the brief provides one, and make the requested
next action understandable. Match the page or message's purpose instead of forcing every draft into
a fixed landing-page template. Offer headline variants only when requested or necessary for a choice.

Keep features, measured outcomes, reported experiences and promises distinct. Do not invent customer
quotes, logos, pricing, guarantees, scarcity, deadlines or comparative superiority. A testimonial
needs a supplied source and faithful wording. A stronger claim needs stronger evidence, not stronger
adjectives. Keep the Humanizer pass active so persuasion still sounds natural and specific.

Return copy in the requested format. Implementing page layout or interactions belongs to /ui;
sending, publishing or altering a live campaign requires authorization for that action.
