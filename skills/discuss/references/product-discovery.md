# Product assumptions and discovery

Read only when discussing or grilling a product, feature, adoption problem or launch decision.
This is an interview aid, not permission to write a PRD, schedule work or run experiments.

1. Separate the desired outcome, the user's problem, a proposed solution and the assumptions
   connecting them. Supplied evidence and user constraints win over framework templates.
2. Examine the relevant risks: value (will it help?), usability (can people use it?), viability
   (can it be operated/supported?) and feasibility (can it be built?). Add distribution or team
   risks only when a new venture makes them material. Do not invent personas, demand or metrics.
3. For each consequential assumption, state supporting evidence, a plausible counterexample,
   confidence and the smallest observation that would change the decision. Distinguish an
   empirical question from a preference the operator can decide.
4. Compare credible alternatives, including the current approach when viable. Prioritize the
   assumptions whose failure would invalidate the most work and whose evidence is weakest.
   Use qualitative rankings unless the inputs justify scoring; label estimates and normalize
   scales before doing arithmetic. Do not manufacture precision or a required number of ideas.
5. For a requested pre-mortem, imagine failure in the actual decision horizon. Separate supported
   risks, disproven concerns and unresolved concerns. For a material risk identify the trigger,
   consequence, possible mitigation and evidence needed. Do not dismiss an uncertain risk as fake
   or invent an owner/deadline. Recommend experiments in chat; execution needs task authorization.

Stop when further discussion depends on new evidence or an operator choice. Do not exhaust every
framework branch or turn the conversation into an automatic task/document pipeline.
